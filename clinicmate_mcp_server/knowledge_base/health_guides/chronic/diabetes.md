# Diabetes

## What is diabetes
Diabetes ("sugar") is a long-term condition in which blood sugar stays too high because the body does not make enough insulin or cannot use it well. Pakistan has one of the highest rates of diabetes in the world, and many people have it without knowing.

## Types
- Type 1: the body makes little or no insulin. Usually starts in children or young adults and needs insulin from the start.
- Type 2: the most common type. The body does not use insulin well. Linked to weight gain, low activity, family history and age.
- Gestational diabetes: high sugar during pregnancy.
- Prediabetes: sugar higher than normal but not yet diabetes. Lifestyle changes at this stage can stop diabetes.

## Symptoms
- Feeling very thirsty and passing urine often, also at night.
- Tiredness and weakness.
- Weight loss without trying (more in type 1).
- Blurred vision.
- Slow-healing wounds, frequent infections, itching in private areas.
- Tingling or numbness in the feet.
Many people with type 2 have no symptoms at first.

## Tests
- Fasting blood sugar.
- Random blood sugar.
- HbA1c: shows the average sugar of the last 2 to 3 months.
Adults over 30 to 35, people who are overweight, and people with a family history should test every year.

## Complications
High sugar over years can damage the eyes, kidneys, nerves, heart and feet. It increases the risk of heart attack and stroke. Good control greatly reduces these risks.

## Living with diabetes
- Take treatment exactly as advised by your doctor and check sugar regularly.
- Eat less sugar, sweets, white bread, white rice and fried food. Eat more vegetables, lentils and whole grains.
- Walk or exercise at least 30 minutes most days.
- Keep a healthy weight and do not smoke.
- Get eyes, kidneys and feet checked every year.

## Foot care
- Check your feet daily for cuts, blisters or colour changes.
- Never walk barefoot; wear comfortable shoes.
- Keep feet clean and dry, and cut nails straight.
- Show any foot wound to a doctor early, even if it does not hurt.

## Emergency signs
Very low sugar (shaking, sweating, confusion, fainting): give something sweet at once if the person can swallow, and call 1122 if they do not improve or are unconscious. Very high sugar with vomiting, deep fast breathing or drowsiness also needs emergency care.

## Common questions
- Sugar ki alamat kya hain? Bohat pyaas, baar baar peshab, thakan, zakhm der se bharna.
- HbA1c kya hai? Pichhle 2 se 3 mahine ki average sugar ka test.
- Sugar ke liye kaun sa doctor? Endocrinologist ya diabetes specialist.
- Pairon ka khayal kyun zaroori hai? Sugar se pairon ki nasain kamzor hoti hain aur zakhm mehsoos nahi hota.
