# Heart Attack Warning Signs

## What is a heart attack
A heart attack happens when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is suddenly blocked, usually by a blood clot in a narrowed artery. Every minute counts: quick treatment saves heart muscle and life.

## Warning signs
- Chest pain, pressure, heaviness or tightness, often in the centre of the chest, lasting more than a few minutes or coming back.
- Pain spreading to the left or both arms, neck, jaw, back or upper stomach.
- Shortness of breath.
- Cold sweat.
- Nausea or vomiting.
- Dizziness, light-headedness or a feeling of fear.

## Signs can be different
Women, elderly people and people with diabetes may not have strong chest pain. They may only feel breathlessness, unusual tiredness, stomach discomfort or nausea. Do not ignore these signs, and do not assume it is gas or acidity.

## What to do immediately
- Call 1122 at once, or get to the nearest hospital emergency quickly.
- Do not drive yourself.
- Sit the person down, loosen tight clothes and keep them calm.
- Do not wait to see if the pain goes away.
- If the person collapses and is not breathing, start chest compressions (CPR) if you know how, until help arrives.

## Risk factors
- High blood pressure, diabetes and high cholesterol.
- Smoking, naswar and tobacco.
- Being overweight and inactive.
- Family history of heart disease at a young age.
- Stress and older age. In South Asia, heart disease often starts at a younger age.

## Prevention
- Check blood pressure, sugar and cholesterol regularly.
- Stop smoking and tobacco.
- Walk at least 30 minutes most days.
- Eat less ghee, fried food and salt; more vegetables, fruit and lentils.
- Keep a healthy weight and manage stress.
