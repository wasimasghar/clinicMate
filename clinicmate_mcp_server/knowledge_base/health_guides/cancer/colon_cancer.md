# Colon (Bowel) Cancer

## What is colon cancer
Colon cancer, also called bowel or colorectal cancer, starts in the large intestine or rectum. It often begins as small growths (polyps) that can be found and removed before they turn into cancer.

## Warning signs
- Blood in the stool or bleeding from the back passage.
- A change in bowel habits lasting more than 3 weeks: looser, more frequent, or constipation.
- Stomach pain or bloating.
- Weight loss without trying.
- Tiredness and paleness (anaemia).
Piles also cause bleeding, but blood in stool should always be checked by a doctor.

## Risk factors
- Age over 45 to 50.
- Family history of bowel cancer or polyps.
- Diet high in red and processed meat and low in fibre.
- Being overweight, inactive, and smoking.
- Long-standing inflammatory bowel disease.

## Screening
Stool tests for hidden blood and colonoscopy (a camera test of the bowel) can find polyps and early cancer. Ask a doctor about screening from about age 45 to 50, or earlier with a family history.

## Prevention
- Eat more fibre: vegetables, fruit, lentils and whole grains.
- Limit red and processed meat.
- Stay active and keep a healthy weight.
- Stop smoking.

## When to see a doctor
See a Gastroenterologist for blood in stool or a lasting change in bowel habits. An Oncologist treats confirmed cancer.
