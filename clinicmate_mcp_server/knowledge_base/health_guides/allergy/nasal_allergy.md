# Nasal Allergy (Allergic Rhinitis)

## What is nasal allergy
Nasal allergy, called allergic rhinitis, happens when the body's defence system over-reacts to harmless things in the air such as pollen or dust. The lining of the nose becomes swollen and makes extra mucus. It is very common in Pakistan, especially in spring in Islamabad and during the smog season in Lahore. It is not contagious.

## Symptoms
- Repeated sneezing, often many times in a row, especially in the morning.
- Runny nose with clear, watery mucus.
- Blocked or stuffy nose, breathing from the mouth, snoring.
- Itching in the nose, throat, ears or roof of the mouth.
- Itchy, red, watery eyes.
- Cough from mucus dripping down the throat.
- Tiredness and poor sleep because of the blocked nose.
- Dark circles under the eyes in children.

## Flu, cold or allergy: how to tell
- Fever: common with flu, sometimes with a cold, NOT with allergy.
- Body aches and weakness: strong with flu, mild with a cold, not with allergy.
- Itchy eyes and nose: common with allergy, rare with flu or cold.
- Mucus: clear and watery in allergy; can become thick, yellow or green in a cold or sinusitis.
- Duration: a cold or flu gets better in about 1 to 2 weeks; allergy lasts as long as you are exposed to the trigger, for weeks or a whole season.
- Pattern: allergy comes back every year in the same season or in the same place (for example only at home or only near dust).
- Contagious: flu and cold spread to others; allergy does not.

## Triggers in the air (allergens)
- Pollen: from trees, grass and weeds. In Islamabad and Rawalpindi, paper mulberry pollen is very high from about February to April and causes severe allergy in many people.
- House dust mites: tiny insects in dust, mattresses, pillows, carpets and curtains. They cause year-round allergy.
- Mould: in damp walls, bathrooms and during the monsoon.
- Animal dander: from cats, dogs, birds and other pets.
- Cockroach droppings.

## Irritants: perfume, smoke and smog
Some things irritate the nose even in people without a true allergy, and they also make allergy worse. This is sometimes called non-allergic rhinitis, and the symptoms look the same.
- Perfume, attar, body spray, deodorant and air freshener.
- Incense (agarbatti), dhoop and strong scented candles.
- Cigarette, shisha, stove and wood smoke.
- Smog and air pollution, very high in Lahore and Punjab from about November to January.
- Strong cleaning chemicals, paint and petrol fumes.
- Cold air and sudden weather change.
If you sneeze or get a blocked nose around perfume or strong smells, avoiding these smells is the most useful step.

## Seasonal and year-round allergy
- Seasonal allergy comes at the same time every year, usually because of pollen in spring or after the monsoon.
- Year-round allergy is usually caused by dust mites, mould, pets or cockroaches at home or at work.
Some people have both.

## Problems if not controlled
- Repeated sinusitis and ear infections.
- Poor sleep, tiredness and poor concentration at work or school.
- Nasal allergy and asthma often go together. Uncontrolled nasal allergy can make asthma worse.

## Tests
An Allergist can find the cause with:
- Skin prick test: small drops of common allergens are placed on the arm with a tiny prick to see which cause a reaction.
- Blood test (specific IgE) for common allergens.
Knowing the exact trigger makes avoiding it much easier.

## Treatment
Treatment is decided by a doctor after examination. Options a doctor may discuss include avoiding triggers, medicines to reduce sneezing and swelling, nose sprays, and for some people allergy shots or drops (immunotherapy) that slowly train the body to stop reacting. Do not use nose decongestant sprays for more than a few days without a doctor's advice, as long use can make the blockage worse.

## Prevention at home
- Wash bedsheets and pillow covers weekly in hot water and dry them in the sun.
- Use dust-mite covers for mattresses and pillows. Reduce carpets and heavy curtains.
- Dust with a damp cloth and vacuum instead of sweeping, or wear a mask while cleaning.
- Keep windows closed on high pollen or high smog days. Check the AQI (air quality index) and stay indoors when it is high.
- Wear a good mask (N95 or similar) outdoors during pollen and smog season.
- Shower and change clothes after coming home in pollen season.
- Avoid perfume, body spray, incense and smoke. Ask family not to smoke inside the house.
- Fix damp walls and leaks to stop mould.
- Keep pets out of the bedroom.

## When to see a doctor
- Symptoms most days for more than 2 weeks, or every year in the same season.
- Allergy affecting sleep, work or school.
- Wheezing, chest tightness or cough at night (may be asthma).
- Facial pain and thick coloured mucus (may be sinusitis).
An Allergist finds the cause. An ENT Specialist helps if the nose stays blocked or there are polyps.

## Emergency signs
Call 1122 or go to emergency at once for difficulty breathing, wheezing that does not settle, swelling of the lips, tongue or face, or feeling faint after contact with something. This can be a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis).

## Common questions
- Subah uthte hi cheenkein aati hain? Ye aksar dust mites ki allergy hoti hai.
- Perfume ya body spray se cheenkein aur naak band? Tez khushbu naak ko irritate karti hai, in se door rahein aur Allergist ko dikhayein.
- Flu hai ya allergy? Bukhar aur jism mein dard ho to flu, khujli aur cheenkein bina bukhar ke ho to allergy.
- Islamabad mein bahar ke mausam mein allergy kyun hoti hai? Paper mulberry ke pollen ki wajah se, February se April tak.
- Smog mein naak aur gala kharab? Mask pehnein, AQI dekh kar bahar niklein.
- Allergy ka test kaise hota hai? Skin prick test ya blood test se.
- Naak ki allergy ke liye kaun sa doctor? Allergist, ya ENT Specialist.
