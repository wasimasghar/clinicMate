# Skin Allergy

## What is skin allergy
Skin allergy is a reaction of the skin to something it touches, something eaten, or sometimes heat, cold or stress. Common types are hives (urticaria), eczema (atopic dermatitis) and contact dermatitis.

## Hives (urticaria)
Raised, itchy, red or skin-coloured patches (wheals) that appear suddenly, move around the body and usually fade within a day. Triggers include foods, insect stings, infections, heat, pressure on the skin and some medicines. Hives that keep coming for more than 6 weeks are called chronic hives.

## Eczema
Dry, itchy, rough skin, often on the face and cheeks in babies and on the elbows, behind the knees and neck in older children and adults. It often runs in families with asthma and nasal allergy. Scratching makes it worse and can cause infection.

## Contact dermatitis
A rash where the skin touched something:
- Perfume, attar, cosmetics, creams and hair dye (including black henna).
- Nickel in jewellery, watch backs and belt buckles.
- Washing powder, detergents and cleaning chemicals.
- Rubber gloves, some plants, and artificial jewellery.
The rash is red, itchy, sometimes with small blisters, and stays in the shape of the contact area.

## Care and prevention
- Find and avoid the trigger. Note what you ate, wore or used before the rash.
- Use mild, fragrance-free soap and moisturiser. Bathe in lukewarm, not hot, water.
- Do not scratch; keep nails short and wear soft cotton clothes.
- Patch test new creams, perfumes or hair dye on a small area first.
- Avoid black henna and cheap artificial jewellery if you react to them.

## When to see a doctor
- Rash that lasts more than a few days, keeps coming back or spreads.
- Eczema that affects sleep or becomes painful, oozing or crusted (may be infected).
- You cannot find what is causing the reaction.
A Dermatologist treats skin allergy. An Allergist can help find the trigger with patch or allergy tests.

## Emergency signs
Call 1122 or go to emergency at once if a rash comes with swelling of the lips, tongue, eyes or throat, difficulty breathing or swallowing, dizziness or fainting. This can be anaphylaxis.

## Common questions
- Jism par achanak lal daane aur khujli? Ye hives (chhapaki) ho sakti hai.
- Perfume ya cream lagane se jild par khujli? Ye contact allergy hai, us cheez ka istemal band karein.
- Mehndi lagane se jild jal gayi? Kaali mehndi mein kemikal hota hai, Dermatologist ko dikhayein.
- Chehra aur honth soojh gaye? Foran emergency jayein.
