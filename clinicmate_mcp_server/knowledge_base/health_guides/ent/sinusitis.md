# Sinusitis

## What is sinusitis
Sinuses are air spaces inside the bones of the face around the nose and eyes. Sinusitis is swelling or infection of the lining of these spaces. It is called acute when it lasts less than 4 weeks and chronic when it lasts more than 12 weeks.

## Causes
- A cold or flu that does not clear.
- Nasal allergy to dust, pollen or smoke.
- Smog and air pollution.
- A bent nose bone (deviated septum) or nasal polyps that block drainage.
- Smoking.

## Symptoms
- Blocked or runny nose with thick yellow or green mucus.
- Pain or pressure in the face, forehead, around the eyes or cheeks, often worse when bending forward.
- Reduced sense of smell.
- Headache, toothache in the upper teeth, bad breath.
- Mucus dripping down the back of the throat, causing cough.
- Mild fever or tiredness.

## Home care and prevention
- Rest and drink plenty of water.
- Steam inhalation and saline (salt water) nose rinses can help clear mucus.
- Avoid smoke, smog and strong smells. Wear a mask on high pollution days.
- Treat nasal allergies, as they often lead to sinusitis.
Most acute sinusitis gets better within 2 to 3 weeks.

## When to see a doctor
- Symptoms lasting more than 10 days, or getting worse after first improving.
- Sinusitis that keeps coming back several times a year.
- Blocked nose on one side only, or bleeding from the nose.
An ENT Specialist can check for polyps or a bent nose bone and advise treatment, including surgery if needed.

## Emergency signs
Go to emergency at once for swelling or redness around the eyes, double vision or changes in vision, severe headache, stiff neck, confusion or high fever.
