# Tuberculosis (TB)

## What is TB
Tuberculosis is a bacterial infection that mostly affects the lungs but can affect other parts of the body. It spreads through the air when a person with lung TB coughs or sneezes. Pakistan is among the countries with the most TB cases in the world. TB is curable.

## Symptoms
- Cough for more than 2 weeks, sometimes with blood in the sputum.
- Fever, especially in the evening.
- Night sweats.
- Weight loss and loss of appetite.
- Tiredness and chest pain.

## Who is at higher risk
- People living with someone who has TB.
- People with diabetes, weak immunity or malnutrition.
- Smokers.
- People living in crowded, poorly ventilated places.

## Tests
A sputum test (GeneXpert or smear) and a chest X-ray are the usual tests. Testing and treatment are available free at government TB programme centres in Pakistan.

## Treatment
TB is treated with a combination of medicines for at least 6 months, prescribed by a doctor. Never stop treatment early, even if you feel better, because incomplete treatment can lead to drug-resistant TB, which is much harder to treat.

## Prevention
- Finish the full treatment so you stop spreading TB.
- Cover your mouth when coughing; people with TB should wear a mask in the first weeks of treatment.
- Keep rooms well ventilated with open windows.
- BCG vaccine is given to newborns in Pakistan's routine immunisation.
- Family members of a TB patient should get checked.

## When to see a doctor
See a Pulmonologist (chest specialist) or General Physician for a cough lasting more than 2 weeks, coughing blood, or weight loss with evening fever.
